# Velocity model — honest evaluation report

First leakage-free evaluation of the **rebuilt** velocity collection (the original
training data was lost; this is the new time-series collected since 2026-06-08).
Generated by `python -m scripts.evaluate_models --compare-splits` (channel-grouped
`GroupKFold`, 5-fold; XGBoost on log-views; baselines = persistence and
persistence × median train-fold growth). Satisfies remediation **Phase 2.2**.

## Run: 2026-06-11 (24,052 videos with snapshots, all with channel_id)

| Horizon | n | channels | model MAPE | R² | best baseline | **lift** | random-split MAPE | leakage gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1h→6h  | 18,245 | 2,382 | 46.7% | 0.773 | 40.9% | **−14.0%** | 63.5% | −16.8pp |
| 1h→24h | 14,431 | 2,229 | 85.7% | 0.575 | 66.7% | **−28.5%** | 80.1% | +5.6pp |
| 1h→3d  | 4,094 | 1,336 | 117.8% | 0.383 | 87.6% | **−34.4%** | 112.3% | +5.5pp |

## Headline finding

**At every currently-measurable horizon, a freshly-trained XGBoost point model
LOSES to the naive "scale current views by median growth" baseline** (every lift
is negative). The high-looking R² (0.43–0.77) next to negative lift is a live
demonstration of the autocorrelation trap remediation Phase 2 warned about:
**R² is the wrong metric; report MAPE + lift.** Random-split MAPE is mostly lower
than grouped (positive leakage gap), confirming the old random-split numbers were
inflated by same-channel contamination — channel-grouped CV is the honest setting.

**→ No velocity point-predictor is shippable today.** The promotion gate
(lift > 0 vs baseline) is not met by any horizon.

## Why, and what it means for the product

Caveats on *why* the model loses (each is a lever, not an excuse):
1. **No channel context.** This run leaves subscriber/channel-trailing-median
   features NaN (the tracked-videos loader doesn't join them yet). A video's
   1h→24h trajectory is largely a channel-baseline story; adding grouped-safe
   channel features is the most likely way to earn positive lift. **Test next.**
2. **Grace-window age noise.** The "1h" label spans `[1h, 3h]` (2h grace), so
   `views_1h` is sampled at variable true ages → a noisy denominator the baseline
   also suffers from. Normalizing by `hours_since_publish` / using `vph` may help.
3. **Point + MAPE is the wrong objective for this product.** MAPE on absolute
   views punishes exactly the breakouts the product exists to flag. The value is
   in (a) **calibrated ranges** (quantile/conformal, plan 6.3), (b) **breakout
   classification** (relative outperformance, plan 7.3 — MAPE doesn't measure it),
   (c) the **reach predictor** on packaging (pre-upload, plan 6.1). This result
   reinforces that pivot rather than contradicting it.

## Experiment: + channel-baseline features (2026-06-11)

Added (a) real subscriber counts (joined from `channel_snapshots` → lights up
`log_subscribers`/`views_per_sub`) and (b) a leakage-safe **leave-one-out
channel-baseline** feature (`chanbase_logviews_{cp}` = mean log-views over the
channel's *other* videos; `views_vs_chanbase_{cp}` = how this video beats it).
Run `--channel-features` (default) vs `--no-channel-features`, same environment:

| Horizon | lift (no channel) | lift (+ channel) | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1h→6h  | −21.4% | −15.0% | +6.4pp |
| 1h→24h | −28.7% | −26.0% | +2.7pp |
| 1h→3d  | −43.9% | −24.9% | **+19.0pp** |
| 6h→24h | −29.8% | −21.6% | +8.2pp |
| 6h→3d  | −39.1% | −18.9% | **+20.2pp** |
| 24h→3d | −17.8% | −15.2% | +2.6pp |

**Conclusion:** channel context is the right lever — it improves every horizon,
by ~20pp at the 3d horizons — but **no horizon beats the baseline yet** (all lifts
still negative). The leave-one-out channel-baseline does the work; raw subscriber
count alone is marginal (the `--no-channel-features` run, which still has subs, is
slightly *worse* than the original NaN-subs report — subs add a little overfitting
noise without the baseline ratio). Persistence×growth remains the thing to serve
and to beat. (Caveat: the baseline's `median_growth` is computed globally incl.
test rows — a tiny edge to the baseline; fold-internal would shrink the gap
marginally but not flip the conclusion.)

## The reframe that works: breakout CLASSIFICATION (2026-06-11)

`scripts/evaluate_breakout.py` — same channel-grouped CV, but the product question:
from EARLY signals (1h/6h), predict whether a video **breaks out** = top quartile
(or decile) of channel-relative overperformance (`views_vs_chanbase`) at 24h/3d.
Metric: ROC-AUC. Baseline to beat: ranking by the single early feature
"views vs the channel's norm" (does early *engagement* add over early *views*).

| Horizon | breakout=top25% model AUC | top10% model AUC | early-views AUC | model Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1h→24h | 0.859 | 0.879 | 0.85–0.86 | +0.010 / +0.015 |
| 6h→24h | **0.940** | **0.948** | 0.93 | +0.012 / +0.016 |
| 1h→3d  | 0.795 | 0.817 | 0.79–0.82 | +0.008 / ~0 |
| 6h→3d  | 0.862 | 0.880 | 0.85–0.88 | +0.008 / ~0 |

**Two findings that matter:**
1. **Breakout is highly predictable from early signals** (AUC 0.86–0.88 at 1h,
   **0.94–0.95 at 6h**) — the *same data + features* that LOSE to baseline on
   point-MAPE WIN decisively as a classifier. This is where the product value is.
   It confirms the plan's pivot: predict *whether it breaks out*, not *how many views*.
2. **Most of the signal is "is this video outpacing its channel's norm early."**
   The single-feature baseline alone hits AUC 0.85–0.93; the full XGBoost adds a
   consistent but small +0.01–0.016. So **a simple, explainable detector is ~99%
   as good as a heavy model** — ship the early-views-vs-channel ratio with a
   threshold (and optionally a light calibrated model for the marginal lift).

**Recommended for 7.3 (breakout detector):** score = early channel-relative views
(`views_vs_chanbase_{from_cp}`); 6h is the sweet spot (AUC ~0.94), 1h gives an
earlier, slightly-less-certain call. Calibrate the probability (6.3) before showing
users "X% chance of breakout". Engagement features are a minor bonus, not the core.

## Per-niche breakout analysis (2026-06-13, plan 8.10 stage 1)

`scripts/evaluate_breakout_by_niche.py` — using the ~100%-coverage
`youtube_category_id` (7.1/8.5), tests whether breakout warrants per-niche
models/calibrators. n=20,953 (6h→24h, top-25%), global grouped-CV AUC 0.933.

| niche | n | within-niche AUC | global ECE (held-out) | per-niche ECE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| News | 10,206 | **0.958** | 0.014 | 0.005 |
| Sports | 1,072 | 0.938 | 0.024 | 0.023 |
| People | 1,691 | 0.918 | 0.017 | 0.011 |
| Entertainment | 1,626 | 0.915 | 0.035 | 0.030 |
| Howto | 585 | 0.890 | 0.039 | 0.038 |
| Education | 999 | 0.873 | 0.040 | 0.037 |
| Science | 668 | 0.859 | 0.045 | 0.042 |
| Gaming | 2,814 | **0.843** | 0.043 | 0.019 |

**Two findings (both honest — AUC is grouped-CV, ECE is held-out OOF):**
1. **Breakout is NOT niche-invariant: AUC 0.843–0.958.** News breakouts are
   highly callable from early signal (fast-moving, the 6h trajectory is
   decisive); Gaming/Science/Education are much less predictable early
   (slower-burn, more late pickups). This is the real, robust result.
2. **Per-niche calibration is marginal.** The GLOBAL isotonic is already
   well-calibrated held-out everywhere (ECE 0.014–0.045); per-niche only
   meaningfully helps Gaming (0.043→0.019) and modestly News/People.

**Verdict (honest-eval discipline — don't ship what doesn't clear the bar):**
do **not** build per-niche classifiers (the single channel-relative feature is
near-ceiling where it works) or per-niche calibrators (global is already
<0.05 ECE). Instead, **surface niche-conditioned detection confidence** — the
measured within-niche AUC is shipped as `NICHE_DETECTION_AUC` in the breakout
detector so the feed/UI can qualify a call ("high confidence: news 0.96" vs
"moderate: gaming 0.84"). Cheap, grounded, and saves building unjustified infra.

## Next actions (gated on this report)
- Add grouped-safe **channel features** (subscriber count, channel trailing-median
  views) to the tracked loader; re-run — does any horizon clear lift > 0?
- Re-run as horizons mature (7d ~Jun 15 … 30d ~Jul 8); later horizons have *more*
  autocorrelation, so the baseline gets *harder* to beat — manage expectations.
- Build the velocity model as **horizon-conditioned quantile + conformal** (5.5),
  evaluated on interval calibration/coverage, not just point MAPE.
- Until a horizon clears the gate, **serve the persistence×growth baseline** (it's
  the thing to beat) with an honest uncertainty range — don't ship a worse model.
