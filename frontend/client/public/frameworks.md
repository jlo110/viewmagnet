## Why frameworks?

Most creators think viral content is about quality. Quality is necessary but not sufficient — the algorithm rewards three mechanical signals: did the viewer start watching, did they keep watching, did they watch again. A framework is just a repeatable structure that engineers all three.

Each of the four below is optimized for a different outcome. Picking the right one for the job matters more than improvising — that's why ViewMagnet's Script Writer asks you what you're trying to do first, then writes against the structure proven to deliver it.

## The 4 Viral Script Frameworks

### 1. Authority Myth-Buster
**Goal: Trust** — Build trust fast with new audiences

When your audience doesn't know who you are yet, you need to earn the right to be listened to in the first 18 seconds.

**Best for:** New audiences, cold reach, channels that need to anchor their authority before delivering value.

**Structure:**
1. **Contrarian Hook** — Open by challenging a widely-held belief in the niche. The viewer's first internal reaction should be "wait, am I doing this wrong?"
2. **Authority Anchor** — Drop credentials within the first 15 seconds — what you've done, built, or measured that gives you the right to challenge that belief.
3. **3-Part Solution** — Three concrete, actionable points. Each builds on or contrasts the previous one. The third should be the most counterintuitive.
4. **Ideological CTA** — Belief-based call to action: "If you believe X, follow for more." This converts trust into followers, not just views.

**Example opening:** "Everyone tells you to post 3 times a week. I built a 2M-follower channel posting twice a month. Here's why frequency is killing your growth…"

---

### 2. Delta Shift
**Goal: Saves** — The highest-save-rate format we measure

Show the gap between what most people do and what the top 1% does. The contrast itself is what makes viewers hit save — "I want to come back to this."

**Best for:** Tactical content where the value is in a specific behavioral upgrade the viewer wants to remember.

**Structure:**
1. **Baseline** — Show what the AVERAGE person does. Be specific so the viewer self-identifies: "Most creators post 3 times a week and…"
2. **Delta (the top 1% move)** — Reveal what the top 1% does instead. The contrast should be sharp and surprising — not a slight tweak.
3. **Single Takeaway** — ONE immediately actionable thing the viewer can apply today. Don't list five — five things never get implemented.
4. **Save CTA** — Direct prompt: "Save this so you don't forget" or "Send this to the creator friend who needs to hear it."

**Example opening:** "Most YouTubers spend 4 hours writing titles. The top 1% spend 4 hours writing the first 3 seconds — here's the one move that doubled my CTR…"

---

### 3. Progressive Debunking (Iceberg)
**Goal: Retention** — Engineered for maximum watch time

Each revelation makes the previous one feel like the surface. The viewer keeps watching because the brain refuses to stop before the deepest layer.

**Best for:** Long-form content, deep dives, polarizing topics where the audience already has strong opinions you can progressively dismantle.

**Structure:**
1. **Polarizing Topic** — Pick a hot debate in the niche and state the controversial topic upfront so the viewer commits to a side.
2. **Dismiss the Surface** — "Everyone obsesses over X. That's noise." Dismiss the surface-level tactics — establishes that you're going somewhere deeper.
3. **Reveal Layer 1** — "The real mechanic is Y." First deeper revelation — what people who've gone one level deeper already understand.
4. **Reveal Layer 2** — "But underneath that is Z." Even deeper. This is what experts think about. By here, the viewer is locked in.
5. **Final Revelation** — The deepest truth — what only the top 0.1% understand. Make it feel like insider knowledge the viewer just earned.
6. **Loop Back** — End by referencing the polarizing topic from the hook so the viewer wants to rewatch with the new context — the loop is what pushes watch time above 100%.

**Example opening:** "Most creators think the algorithm rewards quality. They're wrong — it rewards watch behavior. But underneath that is what TikTok engineers call the 'completion lattice', and underneath THAT is the only signal that actually matters…"

### 4. Lead Magnet Framework
**Goal: Leads** — Fills auto-DM funnels with keyword CTAs

Promise a named system. Tease the deliverable on screen (blurred). Hand it over only when the viewer comments your keyword — which becomes an auto-DM trigger.

**Best for:** Creators with a product, service, or audience to build off-platform. Pure lead-gen mechanic — converts views into DMs into emails.

**Structure:**
1. **Named System Promise** — Promise a memorable, distinctive named method. The name doubles as the comment keyword later, so it has to be specific enough that "comment X" won't pull in unrelated replies.
2. **Blurred Asset Tease** — Show the deliverable on screen, deliberately blurred. The visual proof that the asset exists is what pulls comments — words alone don't.
3. **Rapid Breakdown** — Three quick, punchy parts of the system. Just enough detail to validate that the full asset is worth getting. Hold back the actual playbook.
4. **Keyword CTA** — "Comment [SYSTEM-NAME] and I'll send it to you." The same name from the opening — pattern-completes the whole script and triggers the auto-DM.

**Example opening:** "I built the CLEAR framework to write hooks that 10x'd my views. Full template is blurred on screen right now. Comment CLEAR and I'll DM it to you…"

## The 6 Hook Types (what Hook Lab classifies into)

Every framework above opens with a hook. ViewMagnet's Hook Lab scores your hook against these six types — one weak (Summary) and five strong — and rewrites it toward whichever type fits best.

### Summary *(Weak — avoid)*

Just announces what the video covers. Doesn't open a gap, doesn't make the viewer feel anything is at stake — so the scroll wins.

- **Before:** "Here are 3 tips to grow your Instagram."
- **After:** *(Hook Lab flags this as Summary and offers a rewrite into one of the strong types below.)*

### Mistake Callout *(Strong)*

Accuses the viewer (specifically) of doing something wrong. Triggers the "wait, is that me?" loop — the viewer can't keep scrolling until they know.

- **Before:** "Here's how to post reels better."
- **After:** "You've been posting reels wrong — and here's the proof."

### Belief Destruction *(Strong)*

Attacks a thing the viewer believes is true. Forces them to either defend their belief (engagement) or stay to find out if they've been wrong (watch time).

- **Before:** "The best time to post is important."
- **After:** "Posting times don't matter. Here's what actually does."

### Authority Anchor *(Strong)*

Front-loads your credentials so the contrarian take that follows lands as "expert insight" instead of "random opinion." Best paired with a Belief Destruction or Mistake Callout right after.

- **Before:** "Most creators chase trends."
- **After:** "I've generated 8B views across 60+ industries — and here's why chasing trends is killing your channel."

### Stake Reversal *(Strong)*

Tells the viewer to STOP doing the obvious thing and do the opposite instead. Inverts expectation — that surprise is what holds attention through the first 5 seconds.

- **Before:** "You should batch your content."
- **After:** "Stop batching your content. Do this instead."

### Specificity Hook *(Strong)*

Promises a NAMED, specific system or hidden asset. The named-thing makes it feel concrete instead of generic — and pairs naturally with the Lead Magnet framework's keyword-DM mechanic.

- **Before:** "I have a method that works really well."
- **After:** "The CLEAR framework I use to write hooks — full template blurred on screen right now."

## Why these frameworks work — the research

The frameworks are creator-economy taxonomy, but the *reason* each one works hooks into well-established research:

- **Hooks open curiosity gaps** — Loewenstein (1994), *Psychological Bulletin*. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75 The foundational paper on information-gap theory.
- **High-arousal emotion drives shares** — Berger & Milkman (2012), *Journal of Marketing Research*. https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353 Why awe / anger / amusement out-performs neutral framing for Delta Shift & Authority Myth-Buster.
- **Loss aversion makes stake-reversal hooks feel urgent** — Kahneman & Tversky (1979), *Econometrica*. https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185 Losses are felt ~2× as strongly as equivalent gains.
- **Specific numbers out-credibilize round ones** — Schindler & Yalch (2006), *Advances in Consumer Research*. https://www.acrwebsite.org/volumes/12390/volumes/v33/NA-33 The empirical case for the Specificity hook.
- **Practical-value content gets saved & shared** — Berger (2013), *Contagious* ch. 4. https://jonahberger.com/books/contagious/ Why named systems and screenshot-worthy frameworks (Lead Magnet) over-index on save rate.
